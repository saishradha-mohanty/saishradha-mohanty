About Me

I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, advised by Prof. Anna Green in the SAGE Lab. I work on machine learning for computational biology: biological foundation models, protein and genomic sequence modeling, and AI-driven biological discovery, with a particular interest in representation learning and mechanistic interpretability.

Much of my research is about antimicrobial resistance: predicting drug resistance from bacterial genomes, understanding what protein language models have actually learned, and building agentic systems that help generate and test hypotheses about new resistance mechanisms.

Before the PhD, I completed an MS in Computer Science at UMass Amherst (2024) and a B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Tirupati (2020), where I received the Governor's Medal. In between, I spent two years as a machine learning engineer at Mindbody, building recommender systems and ML infrastructure.

Research

  • Towards Sparse Causal Features for Zero-shot Mutation Effect Prediction in a Protein Language Model
    Saishradha Mohanty, M. Phutela, Anna G. Green
    bioRxiv preprint, 2026 · In submission

    A causal feature-discovery framework for ESM-2 that combines sparse autoencoders with activation patching to find the features driving its zero-shot mutation-effect predictions. Across 67 mutations in the DNAJA1 protein, sparse circuits made of about 0.2% of the identified features recover model behavior in 88% of cases (15% for baselines), and many of these features line up with conserved motifs and 3D residue contacts.

    Preprint
  • BIG-TB: A Benchmark for Prediction and Interpretability of Sequence-Based Machine Learning Using Mycobacterium tuberculosis Genomes
    M. Tasmin*, Saishradha Mohanty*, S. Kulkarni, M. R. Farhat, Anna G. Green  (* equal contribution)
    bioRxiv preprint, 2026 · In submission

    An open benchmark, built with Harvard Medical School, of 17,942 M. tuberculosis genomes with drug-resistance phenotypes across 11 antibiotics. We compare biological foundation models (Evo2, ESM-2) with traditional ML, including on held-out lineages, and check model explanations against clinically established resistance mutations. Strong predictive performance turns out not to guarantee biologically faithful explanations.

    Preprint
  • Unifying Themes in Antibiotic Resistance: Towards Improving Predictive Machine Learning Models of Drug Resistance with Cross-Pathogen Insights
    B. Reimer, H. Poonawala, Saishradha Mohanty, Anna G. Green
    2026 · In submission

    Examines resistance themes that recur across pathogens and how these cross-pathogen insights can improve machine learning models that predict drug resistance.
